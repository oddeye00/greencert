# Signed Green Operators for Certified Neural Training Transitions

This anonymous supplement contains the theorem record, paper source, frozen
protocols and seals, claim-bearing source code, sanitized result records,
independent audits, and figure inputs for the paper. `MANIFEST_SHA256.json`
records both the original source hash cited by the seal chain and the packaged
hash after path/author sanitization.

## Evidence hierarchy

The paper makes five distinct empirical claims and audit layers.

1. **Fresh non-modular transfer.** A prospectively frozen study on the public
   Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer (WDBC) dataset uses 24 fresh seeds,
   a training/trigger/certification split, and 72 prespecified seed-threshold
   cases. It freezes 71 candidates, issues 56 singleton certificates across 22
   seeds, and contains all 56 subsequently revealed persistent events. A
   direct 192-bit one-step outward continuation, independent of the randomized
   Green radius, re-certifies all 56 brackets.
2. **Fresh non-modular signed separation.** A separately sealed 12-seed
   handwritten-digits parity study has 24 prespecified seed-gate cases and a
   structural train/trigger/certification barrier. Signed propagation issues
   seven certificates across six seeds and contains all seven. The matched
   unsigned method issues six: the additional signed-only bracket is exactly
   `[147,147]`, with closure 0.2369 signed versus 13.018 unsigned.
   A separate direct 192-bit exact-real continuation retains all seven signed
   brackets, including the signed-only event, without a Green operator or a
   randomized probe. The same frozen study contains two inaccurate finite
   centerline forecasts; both are rejected by deterministic closure before a
   randomized query.
3. **Prospective fixed-radius Transformer confirmation.** An outcome-sealed
   24-seed modular-addition study
   freezes 23 candidates, issues nine singleton brackets across six seeds, and
   contains all nine revealed events. Its 23 local centerline event clocks are
   exact. The execution amendment and its seal are included rather than hidden.
4. **Independent response-centered Transformer confirmation.** A second
   prospectively sealed 24-seed cohort freezes 19 candidates, issues 11
   singleton brackets across seven seeds, and contains all 11 revealed events.
   The matched fixed-radius rule issues 10; first successful shared-probe powers
   are 1--4 (median 2).
5. **Mechanism and computational audits.** On the matched queried
   Transformer cases, signed propagation issues 9 certificates while the
   scalar Euclidean direction-free finite-window baseline issues 1;
   discarding direction inflates the zero-order response by a median 319.70x.
   A 0--4 sweep ablation and matrix-free benchmarks through 1,008,864
   parameters characterize convergence and cost. Exact block batching gives
   2.60--3.00x speedups on two complete certificate replays and reduces the
   matched million-parameter core projection from 18.28 to 11.37 hours. Exact
   matrix-free products also pass adjoint tests for a 102,400-parameter,
   two-block LayerNorm Transformer with bias-corrected AdamW. These are
   post-seal diagnostics rather than additions to prospective counts. A matched
   online replay preserves the sealed bracket while reducing operator time
   8.03x and end-to-end time 2.19x. A predictable witness policy reconstructs
   all 11 response-centered brackets using 349 rather than 1,882 full-time
   output queries. Across sealed horizons 26, 52, 94, and 142, direct-sum role
   fusion preserves all four brackets without fallback, reduces pair work
   33.7%, and gives 1.37x/1.35x aggregate output-operator/output-wall speedups
   (1.11x when shared centerline time is included). The longest-case naive
   separate-role implementation is 0.88x, exposing the launch overhead that
   fusion removes. A monolithic online-Green plus role-fused replay preserves
   `[2,2]`, cuts pair work 22.37%, and improves matched complete time 1.018x,
   localizing centerline/Green work as the remaining bottleneck. On the same
   immutable case, actual float32 q=1 kernels preserve the bracket, run a pooled
   median 1.83x faster across 20 matched pairs in four separately launched
   alternating-order invocations (range 1.04--2.38x), and
   have maximum measured residual ratio `3.36e-7` versus the
   independently computed admissible ratio `0.5999`. Response-centered output
   transport retains all 11 v3 brackets at the same powers while reducing the
   maximum output-radius ratio to 0.091--0.602 (median 0.365), with deterministic
   forward evaluations and no additional randomized query. The historical
   fixed-q8 paper batch takes a median 58.02 minutes per candidate versus 5.31
   seconds for 300-step continuation; this 655.8x disadvantage describes the
   historical implementation. A complete composition on the sealed H=26
   seed-366 case now reproduces `[2,2]` in 9.01--11.71 seconds (9.21-second
   median) with four forward Green probes, zero transpose probes, and zero
   randomized output operators. Matched continuation remains faster at 0.298
   seconds for 26 updates and 3.718 seconds for 300.
   A cancellation-safe directional second response replaces the scalar
   quadratic forcing charge by a signed causal response plus explicit
   fourth-order and arithmetic residuals. In an outcome-blind post-seal audit,
   it closes all 15 Green-evaluable v3 records rather than 11, converts four
   prior abstentions, advances five existing closures, and removes 22
   progressive powers. The adaptive policy invokes it in 9/15 cases; all nine
   checkpoint-local fourth-order gates pass with at least 55.3x residual
   headroom. On sealed seed 366 at the 70% gate (H=52), four alternating-order
   pairs time the directional branch at 78.58 seconds median versus 233.45
   seconds for one additional 16-probe Gram power: 2.91x median, range
   2.77--3.28x. The amplified-secant corollary then removes third-order AD by
   evaluating the existing map/JVP at a power-of-two displacement. At
   lambda=4096 on the same sealed H=52 case it reproduces `[28,28]`, retains
   24.4x analytic headroom, and takes 6.56 seconds median versus 8.10 seconds
   for the third-product branch and 26.01 seconds for the next Gram power:
   4.04x paired median. An independent outcome-blind audit reproduces every
   closure, checksum, and ratio. A subsequent protocol freezes four fresh
   probes and omits the second Green solve. V2 evaluates all 204 scalar secant
   jets in 192-bit Arb, forms state-probe differences inside Arb, bounds the
   exact forcing by 1.3055e-29, retains `[28,28]` with 24.4136x headroom, and
   takes 9.80 minutes wall time on four workers. Twelve randomized
    architecture-level tests match PyTorch through mixed order within 6.67e-16;
    an independent auditor re-sums every interval. These are post-seal
    method-development results; prospective counts do not change. Corrected-path
    relinearization then rebuilds the Jacobian sequence at the signed-response
    center and removes the old mixed closure term. A frozen literal-map audit is
    retained as a negative result: float64 subtraction inflated the corrected
    defect to `3.10e-15` and failed closure. The cancellation-safe amplified
    interface instead retains `[28,28]`; with 16 probes it has 173.73x forcing
    headroom. A separately frozen four-probe reduction retains the bracket with
    2.0028x headroom, reducing Green Gram applications from 16 to four. Three
    alternating matched pairs take 6.51 versus 23.64 seconds (3.63x median,
    3.48--4.10x range). An independent auditor regenerates all four Gaussian
    vectors and operator products. Upstream response/Green/output arithmetic
    remains float64/high-confidence, so this is not promoted to a complete
    computer-assisted Transformer proof.

    The scaled-momentum theorem then factors every nonlinear remainder as
    `Bq=(-eta*q,+eta*q)` and closes the anchored recurrence directly in the
    parameter sequence, with the one-step causal shift made explicit. A sealed
    15-case audit preserves every inherited bracket and reduces staged logical
    Green sweeps from 112 to 96; its independent verifier checks 17 dependency
    hashes and all 15 caches. A second sealed audit removes the identically zero
    anchor forcing block. It again preserves all 15 brackets but remains at 96
    sweeps, so its prespecified strict-improvement gate fails. The negative
    result and its 23-hash independent replay are retained in full.

The WDBC matched unsigned baseline also issues 56/56. This is reported
explicitly: WDBC establishes transfer, information separation, and numerical
rigor; digits gives a protocol-primary non-modular signed-only result; the
Transformer supplies repeated signed-only issuance under attention and momentum.

## Claim boundary

The certificates concern deterministic persistent first-passage events on a
fixed finite certification set along one realized optimization trajectory.
They are not population generalization guarantees, universal grokking
predictors, or certificates for arbitrary large architectures. Randomized
Green-operator bounds for digits and the Transformer are high-confidence
numerical statements under their committed PRNG models. The WDBC and digits
post-seal Arb continuations separately close the floating-point gap for all 63
issued non-modular events without depending on a randomized Green event. Their
exact-real scope begins at stored dyadic checkpoints; they do not verify the
preceding PyTorch/BLAS training execution. Transformer certificates remain
high-confidence statements under the committed ideal-Gaussian/PRNG model:
calibration and scalar roots are outward-audited conditional on stored `Y`,
while the inexact theorem supplies a rigorous interface for future verified
HVP/VJP residuals; norm accumulation, jets, and margins remain unenclosed.
Block batching improves cost without changing the theorem, but the
million-parameter H=300 core currently takes 11.37 hours before jet
construction. Anytime stopping, role-separated confidence allocation, and
witness-sparse event transport target distinct operator-count multipliers. The
